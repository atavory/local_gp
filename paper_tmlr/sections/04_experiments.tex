\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We organize the evidence around three questions.

\begin{enumerate}
\item Does a probe contain label content beyond exchangeable stochastic
filtering?
\item Under fixed worker compute, can deliberately diverse aggregation beat
external HPO rather than only default baselines?
\item Does the mechanism survive outside one local-SGD ledger?
\end{enumerate}

All margins are reported as internal controller minus comparator, so positive
numbers favor \method. Intervals are standard-error-style uncertainty intervals
from the experiment ledgers. The anonymized repository contains the scripts and
run artifacts: \url{https://anonymous.4open.science/r/local_gp-9F83/}.

\subsection{Null and label-content controls}

Table~\ref{tab:null} separates stochastic filtering from HPO. E087 assigns
exchangeable fake labels. The selector improves over uniform averaging, but
the weights have high entropy and low label concentration; this is evidence
that probes can filter realized stochastic updates, not that fake labels carry
hyperparameter information. Shuffled-probe comparisons in later tables are the
main label-content control.

\subsection{Fixed-compute external HPO}

Table~\ref{tab:fixed-compute} is the main local-SGD evidence. E090B constructs
server-side outer-control candidates from a common worker-update pool, probes
them, and applies the chosen/weighted update. It beats the stale/default
control, shuffled probes, validation-selected external HPO, and even the
external oracle in the main pooled setting. E091 and E093 reproduce the result
on fresh and 64-seed runs. E094 clears the longer-round setting. E095 extends
the win to $K=16$, where margins are smaller and the oracle comparison is no
longer positive.

We also include the boundary rows. E089 shows that grouped learning-rate arms
carry label content but do not beat validation-selected external HPO. E090
shows that a stricter stale-default final-trajectory guard beats shuffled
probes but does not clear the stale/default comparator. These rows prevent the
paper from claiming that any probe-weighted aggregation is enough.

\subsection{Language-model tuning}

The language-model experiments are deliberately small and are used as
task-family plausibility, not as a claim about frontier-scale pretraining.
E096 uses soft probe-weighted LR aggregation and is a boundary: it beats
default and shuffled controls but loses to validation-selected external HPO.
E097 switches to one-hot selection and ties the validation-selected external
fixed-LR arm. E099 introduces a shifted text stream where same-round probe
selection beats default, shuffled probes, and validation-selected external
fixed-LR HPO. E102 broadens the adaptive LM evidence to multiple model
families: Transformer-small and GRU character-level causal LMs trained under
adversarial textual shifts from substitution-cipher target text to real
target-domain code or theorem/math text. This is still small-scale, but it is
no longer a single Transformer/code anecdote.

\subsection{Multi-domain adaptive HPO}

The broadest current evidence is the adaptive shift suite in
Table~\ref{tab:multidomain}. E100 spans tabular classification, tabular
regression, PFN-style tabular in-context classification, time-series
forecasting, and synthetic 8-by-8 vision. E101 adds multiclass tabular,
sparse text-like bags, pairwise ranking, graph/node summaries, sequence
classification, and matrix-interaction regression. The E101 low-probe rerun
keeps the win at 8.33\% overhead and all six added domains clear individually.

\subsection{Summary}

The empirical picture is consistent with the analysis. Exchangeable fake arms
can be filtered, but they are not HPO. Deliberately diverse arms produce
repeatable label-content gains over shuffled probes. The strongest evidence is
the fixed-compute outer-control packet, where internal aggregation beats
external HPO without giving itself extra worker compute. The adaptive LM and
multi-domain experiments extend the mechanism to shifted settings where a
fixed validation-selected arm is less able to track the current round.
