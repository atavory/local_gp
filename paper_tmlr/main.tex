\begin{abstract}
Hyperparameter optimization for distributed training is usually external to
the training run: launch several jobs, select a winner, and discard the losing
trajectories. Standard distributed aggregation makes the opposite assumption,
treating worker updates as exchangeable estimates for one optimizer setting. We
study deliberately diverse aggregation, where a distributed round creates a
small set of optimizer or hyperparameter arms, cheap probes score the candidate
next states, and the server selects or aggregates the scored candidates into
one shared trajectory. This places exploration and exploitation inside the
aggregation rule rather than outside the training run. We show that if workers
and data are truly exchangeable, probe weights can only exploit finite-sample
stochastic filtering; persistent HPO signal requires deliberate
non-exchangeability. We then evaluate worker learning-rate arms, server-side
outer-control arms, language-model domain-shift tuning, and synthetic
multi-domain adaptive tuning. Across fixed-compute local-SGD ledgers, the
method beats shuffled probes, default controls, and validation-selected
external HPO; across adaptive shift audits it also beats external fixed-arm HPO
with probe overhead as low as 8.33\%. An anonymized artifact is available at
\url{https://anonymous.4open.science/r/local_gp-9F83/}.
\end{abstract}
